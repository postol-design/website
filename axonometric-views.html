Axonometric interior views

An axonometric view shows how rooms connect — layout, furniture and finishes in one image, without perspective distortion. Clients understand a whole floor in seconds.

When to choose an axonometric view

Choose an axonometric view when your client needs to see how spaces work together: the layout, the furniture and the finishes of several rooms or a whole floor in one image. Choose a perspective rendering when the client needs to feel one room at eye level — its light, materials and mood. Many designers order both, from the same 3D model.

Questions

Axonometric view or 3D floor plan — what's the difference?

A 3D floor plan shows the whole layout from directly above. An axonometric view is turned at an angle, so walls, furniture and finishes read more like real rooms.

Will it match my perspective renderings?

Yes. Every view of a project comes from the same 3D model, so proportions, materials and light stay consistent.

What file will I get?

A high-resolution image at A4, 450 dpi (about 5,260 × 3,720 px), sharp enough for print and large screens.

How fast is it?

Up to 3 days for one view, after the 3D model (up to 1 day). Need it sooner? Express is twice as fast for +50%.
